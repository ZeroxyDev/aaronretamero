There’s something very strange about how we relate to each other now.

I don’t know how to explain it without sounding intense, but more and more I feel like many people don’t really know themselves anymore.

They measure each other.

And I’m not saying that as if I were above it all.

I’m not.

But I also don’t want to lie to myself: I don’t usually live waiting to see if someone takes longer or shorter to reply, or whether they watched a story, or if they answer in five minutes or three hours.

That part matters to me less and less.

What bothers me is something else.

What gets to me is noticing the kind of dynamic I’m stepping into.

It gets to me when a conversation doesn’t feel like a conversation, but like a small test to see what place each person occupies.

When something that should be simple starts to smell like a transaction.

When someone doesn’t ask for something, but hints just enough that you feel pushed to offer it.

When someone doesn’t show clear interest, but leaves doors open because it’s useful for them that those doors exist.

When someone doesn’t want to meet you so much as check what they can provoke in you.

And my part in all this also gets to me.

Because I’m not a clean machine.

I can want something and then start looking for arguments to get closer.

I can convince myself a door is normal when deep down I know the context isn’t entirely clean.

I can confuse intuition with defense, judgment with fear, desire with ego.

And maybe that’s the most uncomfortable thing.

That the system isn’t only outside.

It’s inside too.

Before a conversation is a conversation, many times there’s an invisible economy already running underneath.

Need disguises itself as indifference.

Interest gets administered.

Attention is granted, withdrawn, measured.

People try to look intact while they search for ways to provoke in another: a reaction, a little desire, a touch of jealousy, a small confirmation that they can still affect someone.

We live in a kind of theatre of not needing.

Everyone wants to look free.

As if they expect nothing.

As if they aren’t affected by anything.

As if they don’t calculate.

But then almost everything revolves around producing an effect.

And the worst part is that almost nobody says it outright, because saying it would break the theatre.

It’s more comfortable to call it 'flowing.'

To call it 'let’s see what happens.'

To call it 'I’m not looking for anything.'

To call it 'that’s just how I am.'

Anything before admitting that many times we’re not trying to get to know someone, but to check how much value we have through the reaction we produce in them.

That seems pretty sad to me.

Because in theory we’re more connected than ever.

You can write to almost anyone.

You can see what they do.

You can reply to a story.

You can enter through a small, apparently normal door.

But often that door doesn’t lead to a person.

It leads to a system.

A system of attention.

Of perception.

Of administered desire.

A system where people learn to present themselves in a specific way to produce a specific outcome in others.

And that’s where everything gets dirty for me.

Not dirty in a moralizing way.

Dirty in the sense that it stops feeling human.

Because you no longer know whether someone wants to talk to you or wants to feel desired through you.

You don’t know if someone is sharing something or dropping a piece to see who picks it up.

You don’t know if a line is casual or placed to provoke a question.

You don’t know if a vulnerability is real or being used as a tool.

You don’t know if a person is opening up or designing a scene where you have to take a role.

And I know this might sound paranoid.

That’s why I stop myself.

I don’t want to live suspecting everyone.

I don’t want to turn every conversation into an autopsy.

I don’t want to see strategy where there’s only clumsiness, nor manipulation where there’s only insecurity.

But it’s also hard for me to pretend not to see certain things when they repeat.

An isolated sign can be anything.

A line can be clumsy.

A gesture can be casual.

A change in tone can mean nothing.

But when several things start pointing in the same direction, something inside me withdraws.

You don’t need a big proof.

You don’t need a huge betrayal.

Sometimes it’s enough to notice that behind an interaction that should be simple a transaction begins to appear.

Attention for validation.

Ambiguity for pursuit.

Desire for self-esteem.

Vulnerability for control.

Flirting for resource.

Silence for power.

Interest for utility.

And it’s tiring.

It’s very tiring to detect transactions where you expected humanity.

Because you don’t want to think like that.

You want someone to like you, and that’s it.

You want to talk, and that’s it.

You want a conversation to be a conversation.

You want a person to be a person, not a social interface full of layers.

But sometimes it seems that before you reach someone you have to get through their character, their display window, their way of managing themselves, their need to be perceived, their fear of seeming available, their hunger for attention, their theatre of independence.

And then I ask myself a pretty simple question, but one that unsettles me.

Where is my mind?

Really, where is my mind in the middle of all this?

Because sometimes I don’t know if I’m looking at a person or at everything that person activates inside me.

I don’t know if there’s real interest or ego.

I don’t know if there’s intuition or defense.

I don’t know if I’m seeing a dynamic or if I’m looking for a neat reason not to expose myself.

I don’t know if I want to get to know someone or I simply don’t want to feel replaceable.

I don’t know if I’m attracted to the person or to the image they’ve learned to build to be desired.

And that’s the shitty part.

It would be very easy to write all this from above.

As if I were the lucid one and everyone else were lost in the attention market.

But no.

I’m inside.

I’ve also felt desire trying to negotiate with my judgment.

I’ve also wanted to get close to something and, at the same time, noticed that the path to doing so could dirty me inside.

I’ve also looked for elegant explanations for impulses that perhaps didn’t come from a very clean place.

I’ve also noticed how ego disguises itself as intuition when something attracts me too much.

That’s why it makes me so uncomfortable.

Because I’m not looking at an external problem.

I’m looking at a social dynamic we all participate in, even when we’re disgusted by participating.

The difference is that some people seem comfortable there.

I’m not.

It’s hard for me.

I don’t get bothered by not knowing if someone likes me.

What gets to me is not knowing what kind of game I’m entering to find out.

It’s hard for me to get close when I feel that, before reaching the person, I have to get through too many layers of social theatre.

It’s not hard for me to talk.

It’s hard for me to enter a dynamic where my intention can end up looking like something I myself reject.

It’s hard for me to show interest in a place where showing interest seems to lose power.

It’s hard for me to be in an environment where many people want to be desired, but almost nobody wants to be truly seen.

Because being seen is not the same as being desired.

Being desired can be superficial.

It can be aesthetic.

It can be quick.

It can inflate the ego without touching anything deep.

Being seen is something else.

Being seen means someone gets past the persona a little.

Noticing what you’re not saying.

Not only reacting to your image, but to your way of being in the world.

Not using you as a mirror, but actually meeting you.

And I think that’s getting rarer and rarer.

Not because people are bad.

But because the system rewards something else.

It rewards looking interesting.

Generating desire.

Not needing.

Having options.

Managing attention.

Being visible, but not necessarily real.

So many people learn to live that way.

Not as villains.

As survivors of a strange environment.

They learn to be liked before they learn to bond.

To provoke before they express.

To seem free before they’re honest.

To hold an image before they hold a conversation.

And I suppose we all do some of that.

The problem is when you no longer know where the person ends and the mechanism begins.

When someone speaks to you and a part of you doesn’t just hear what they say, but what they’re trying to produce with it.

When a line doesn’t feel like a line, but like a hook.

When a confession doesn’t feel like intimacy, but like a tool to gain position.

When a joke doesn’t feel like play, but like a way to measure how much power someone has over you.

And something breaks a little there.

Because maybe the person hasn’t done anything terrible.

Maybe there’s no big accusation possible.

Maybe, said out loud, it even sounds exaggerated.

But you noticed something.

A direction.

A way of moving.

A small structure repeating itself.

And when you see a structure, it’s hard to go back to seeing only coincidences.

That happens to me a lot.

I see something and a part of me tries to be fair.

Maybe it isn’t that serious.

Maybe I’m misreading it.

Maybe it’s just insecurity.

Maybe that person doesn’t even know what they’re doing.

And often that’s true.

But other times it’s not.

Other times you notice the conversation isn’t looking for truth, it’s looking for effect.

It’s not looking for meeting, it’s looking for reaction.

It’s not looking to be with you, it’s looking to see what it can provoke in you.

And then the interest lowers.

Not suddenly.

Not as a theatrical decision.

It dims like a light.

Little by little.

Until something that once sparked curiosity starts to feel tiring.

Sometimes we think we stopped feeling out of pride, out of fear or coldness.

But sometimes we stop feeling because something inside has understood that where we expected a person there was too much strategy.

Too much calculation.

Too much image management.

Too much ambiguity used as a tool.

Too much theatre for something that should be simple.

And I’m not saying people have to be perfect.

I’m not perfect.

Nobody is.

We all have ego.

We all have wounds.

We all have strange ways of protecting ourselves.

But there’s a difference between having human contradictions and turning every interaction into a little negotiation of power.

One thing is being insecure.

Another is living by administering other people’s desire as if it were currency.

And I think that’s the phrase that weighs on me the most:

desire has become currency.

It’s exchanged.

It’s measured.

It’s provoked.

It’s withdrawn.

It’s used to raise value.

It’s used to avoid feeling small.

It’s used to check that you still matter.

And of course, when you see that too often, getting close to someone stops being simple.

It’s no longer just "I feel like talking to you."

It’s something else.

From where am I speaking to you?

What game am I entering?

What place will I occupy if I enter?

Am I a person to you or a useful reaction?

Am I seeing humanity or am I entering another invisible economy?

And yes, maybe I overthink.

Probably.

But I also think some things only seem exaggerated until you see them repeat enough times.

Once can be nothing.

Two can be coincidence.

Three already start to speak.

And when something starts to speak, it’s hard to pretend you don’t hear it.

I don’t want to become cold.

That’s what scares me the most.

I don’t want seeing systems to keep me from seeing people.

I don’t want detecting patterns to make me incapable of trusting.

I don’t want my head to turn every gesture into a test.

But I also don’t want to force myself to call humanity what feels like a transaction.

I don’t want to play the fool to maintain an illusion.

I don’t want to enter games just because everyone else seems to accept them.

I don’t want to normalize that a bond begins as a silent competition to see who needs less.

Because to me that does not look like freedom.

It looks like fear with good aesthetics.

Fear of needing.

Fear of exposing oneself.

Fear of seeming intense.

Fear of being rejected.

Fear of not having options.

Fear of being just one more.

Fear that someone will see how much something matters to you.

And then we all act like nothing’s happening.

Like we’re light.

Like nothing matters.

Like nobody expects anything.

But underneath there’s noise.

A lot of noise.

And sometimes that noise dresses up as desire.

Sometimes it passes as freedom.

Sometimes it masquerades as 'that’s how I am.'

Sometimes it calls itself independence, when really it’s just a more sophisticated way to ask for validation without seeming to need it.

And that seems to me one of the saddest things of all.

That many people don’t necessarily want to love, to know, or to build.

They want to feel desired without being exposed.

They want to provoke without asking.

To receive without owing.

To open doors without crossing them.

To have impact without responsibility.

To be looked at without being seen.

And maybe we’ve all been there at some point.

But when you detect it from the outside, it’s tiring.

Tiring because you know that if you enter, you probably don’t enter a story.

You enter a dynamic.

And dynamics eat people.

You start wanting to know someone and end up measuring the role you occupy.

You start curious and end up wondering if you’re just another option.

You start looking for something human and end up participating in an economy you never wanted to accept.

Maybe that’s why I value calm more and more.

Clarity.

People who don’t have to turn everything into tension.

People who don’t have to constantly prove their worth.

People who don’t play at seeming less interested than they are.

People who can talk without turning every line into a tool.

People who can be liked without turning your reaction into their food.

I don’t know if that’s asking too much.

Sometimes it seems like it is.

But I also won’t lower my idea of humanity just because the environment rewards the opposite.

I don’t want to become naive.

But I also don’t want to become cynical.

I suppose the balance is to look without hardening.

To detect without condemning too early.

To protect yourself without turning everything into suspicion.

To accept that some signs aren’t absolute proof, but they’re not nothing either.

And above all, not to betray myself when something inside me has already understood that a dynamic doesn’t give me peace.

Because in the end maybe it all comes down to that.

To peace.

Not to being right.

Not to winning.

Not to coming out on top.

Not to proving you saw something before others.

Just to being able to be near someone without feeling like I have to decode an entire system to know if there’s a person behind it.

That’s what I’m looking for.

A way to get closer that doesn’t feel like competing.

A conversation that doesn’t feel like a test of worth.

A desire that doesn’t feel like currency.

A presence that doesn’t look like a strategy.

Someone who doesn’t need to turn my attention into their mirror.

And maybe that’s why it’s hard for me to get close.

Not because I don’t want to meet people.

But because more and more I notice sooner when the path to someone starts to look more like a system than an encounter.
